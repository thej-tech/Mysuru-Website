The Royal Legacy of Mysuru

Mysuru has a long association with the Wadiyar dynasty and the former Kingdom of Mysore. For centuries, the city served as an important centre of royal administration in southern India.

The city's palaces, temples, markets and public spaces continue to reflect its royal past, while museums and cultural institutions preserve its art and history.

The Wadiyars

The Wadiyar dynasty played an important role in the development of Mysuru as a centre of administration, culture and the arts.

Their patronage contributed to the city's architecture, music, education and cultural traditions. The royal court supported artists and musicians, helping shape a cultural identity that remains visible today.

Mysore Palace

A Symbol of Mysuru

The Mysore Palace is one of the most recognizable landmarks in the city. Its architecture combines different artistic influences and makes the palace a major attraction for visitors. After the earlier palace was destroyed by fire in 1897, the present building was completed in 1912 to designs by architect Henry Irwin.

A Living Heritage

Architecture

Palaces, heritage buildings and historic streets preserve the visual character of old Mysuru. The palace's domes, arches and carved details are among the city's best-known architectural features.

Arts

Mysuru has long been associated with painting, music, crafts and traditional artistic practices. Mysore painting is known for delicate lines, rich colours and detailed work with gold foil.

Festivals

Mysuru Dasara continues to be one of the city's most important cultural celebrations. The multi-day festival features processions, performances and illuminated public spaces.
